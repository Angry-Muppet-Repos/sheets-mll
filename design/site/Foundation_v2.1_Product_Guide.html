Welcome

A premium household budget system. Yours forever, on your own Drive. No subscription, no third party holding your data.

You already know something's off — the dining line keeps climbing, the savings rate stalled in March, you're not sure why. The Foundation is the budget system that gives you the read in two clicks and the controls to do something about it.

Twelve tabs, twenty-four color palettes, ten budget profiles, one bank-CSV importer that recognizes nine US banks out of the box, and a hidden _Schema tab that lets Claude or ChatGPT read your data and tell you what to fix.

This guide is a tour. The first five pages get you operational in ten minutes. The rest is reference — read what you need, ignore what you don't.

Setup Guide · Step 1 of 6

Install the script

This is the only technical step. Go slowly — every click is listed, and you only do it once.

  1. In Google Drive, find the_foundation_v2_BLANK.xlsx from your download. Right-click it and choose Open with → Google Sheets.
  2. In the top menu, click File → Save as Google Sheets. A fresh copy opens — use that copy from now on.
  3. Click Extensions → Apps Script. A new browser tab opens — this is the script editor.
  4. Click inside the Code.gs box, select all (Ctrl/Cmd + A), and delete it.
  5. For each .gs file in your download: click the + next to “Files”, choose Script, name it to match the file, and paste that file’s contents.
  6. Click + → HTML, name it Help, and paste in the included Help.html. Then press Ctrl/Cmd + S to save.
  7. Switch back to your sheet’s tab and refresh the page.
Extensions → Apps Script, then paste the files into the editor.
NoteA 💳 Column & Co. menu now appears just after Help, at the right end of the menu bar. Your first click on it asks for permission — that’s normal. The scope is spreadsheets.currentonly, the narrowest Google offers (it can only touch this one file). Click Allow.

Add your accounts

Tell the sheet about your real accounts so balances and Net Worth are yours.

  1. Click the Accounts tab along the bottom of the sheet.
  2. Add one row per account — checking, credit card, savings, loan. Fill in Name, Type, Owner, and the current Balance.
  3. Leave the Last Updated column alone — it stamps itself whenever you change a balance.
  4. Put investments (401(k), IRA, brokerage) on the Net Worth tab, not here.
The Accounts tab — one row per account.
NoteShortcut: 💳 → Add Account… adds a new row with sensible defaults already filled in.

Import your first month

Bring in real transactions straight from your bank — no typing.

  1. On your bank’s website, download a CSV of about 30 days of activity.
  2. Click the Bank Import Guide tab. In cell C10, pick the matching account from the dropdown.
  3. Click anywhere in the green zone and paste your CSV (Ctrl/Cmd + V).
  4. Run 💳 → Import Bank Transactions.
  5. The cursor jumps to anything needing you: confirm income in Review Income, or pick a category in Uncategorized Merchants (that saves a rule for next time).
Bank Import Guide — 💳 → Import Bank Transactions.
NoteNine banks are recognized automatically (Chase, BoA, Wells, Cap One, Ally, Citi, USAA, Discover, Amex). Re-importing is safe — duplicates are skipped.

Menu Reference · 💳 Column & Co.

Menu Reference

Every item in the 💳 Column & Co. menu — what it does, when to reach for it, what it changes, and the one thing to watch.

The 💳 Column & Co. menu, open.
Add Account…
What it does
Adds a fresh row to the Accounts tab pre-filled with sensible defaults — Type set to Checking, Owner set to Joint, today's date in Last Updated.
When to use it
When you're setting up and don't want to format a new row by hand.
What it touches
The Accounts tab only. Doesn't touch the ledger.
Gotcha
It assumes Joint ownership. Change the Owner cell if it's a personal account.
Import Bank Transactions
What it does
Parses the CSV in the Bank Import Guide paste zone, sniffs the bank format, applies your keyword rules, dedupes against the ledger, and appends to Transactions. Income rows surface in the Review block.
When to use it
Every time you've pasted a month of bank activity and want it categorized and filed.
What it touches
Reads the paste zone and the Categories rules; writes to Transactions and the Review Income block.
Gotcha
The account name in C10 must exactly match an entry on the Accounts tab, or the rows file with a blank account.
Clear Paste Zone
What it does
Wipes the paste zone back to the default 5-row placeholder CSV. Asks for confirmation first.
When to use it
After a successful import, to reset the zone for next month.
What it touches
The Bank Import Guide paste-zone cell only.
Gotcha
It clears whatever's pasted — run your import first, or you'll lose the rows you just pasted.

Recategorize Ledger from Rules
What it does
Re-runs the Categories keyword rules across the entire Transactions ledger. Any row currently in Misc that now matches a rule gets recategorized.

After you've added or edited keyword rules and want them applied retroactively.

Recipes · Chapter 1

Import your first month

From a bank export to a categorized, filed month — in under three minutes.

  1. Open the Bank Import Guide tab and pick your account.
    In cell C10, choose the matching Accounts entry — say Chase Joint Checking. The name must match an Accounts row exactly, or rows land with a blank account.
  2. Copy your whole export and paste it into the green zone.
    Open the bank's CSV, select all, copy, then paste anywhere in the green paste area. The header row is how the script recognizes which of the nine banks it came from.
  3. Run 💳 Column & Co. → Import Bank Transactions.
    A toast confirms the count — Imported 47 transactions. The importer dedupes against the ledger, so re-pasting the same month won't double it.
  4. Categorize the Uncategorized Merchants the cursor jumps to.
    Pick a Category for each new merchant. Every choice saves a keyword rule and back-fills past rows that were filed as Misc.
  5. Skim Review Income for anything flagged positive.
    Confirm Yes/No on the flagged rows. Once cleared, the Dashboard and Trends tabs already reflect the new month.
You'll need

A CSV export from any of the nine supported banks (we'll use Chase here), and that account already added on the Accounts tab.

What you'll have
  • 47 transactions in your ledger
  • 6 new keyword rules saved
  • A clean Review queue
  • Every downstream tab updated
Watch out

Everything filed as Misc means the bank's header row wasn't recognized. Confirm the CSV has a real header, add keyword rules on Categories, then run Recategorize Ledger from Rules.

Recipes · Chapter 2

Swap your look — themes and profiles

Two independent dials: how the sheet looks, and how you budget.

  1. Run 💳 → Apply Theme and pick a palette.
    Any of the 24 palettes retints the sheet content in place. Your data, logo, and layout never move.
  2. Watch the whole sheet repaint.
    The header bar, sub-band, and gold rule all take the palette's colors — the full 3-row header chrome included. Only the wordmark stays fixed.
  3. Run 💳 → Apply Budget Profile and choose a methodology.
    Pick one — FIRE, say — and all 20 category targets rewrite to that method's split.
  4. Switch profiles freely; your edits are remembered.
    Each profile keeps its own overrides. Switch away and back and your custom numbers are still there — they only clear if you run Clear overrides.
You'll need

Nothing. Both are safe to change as often as you like.

What you'll have
  • A palette that suits you
  • A budget methodology applied
  • Header retints to match
  • Per-profile overrides preserved
Watch out

White-accent palettes — Crimson & White and Forest & White — are intentionally low-contrast on a near-white background. Pick a different palette if you want more punch.

Recipes · Chapter 3

Ask Claude or ChatGPT to read your sheet

The AI-ready wedge, in practice — insights in seconds, no formulas.

  1. Copy the starter prompt from the Start Here tab.
    It lives in the AI callout block and primes the assistant on how the workbook is structured.
  2. Paste it into your assistant and attach the sheet.
    Either upload the file, or paste the contents of the _Schema tab so the assistant knows what every column means.
  3. Ask a real question.
    For example: "Find every category trending over budget three-plus months in a row. Estimate the annual cost. List the top three subscriptions to cancel."
  4. Bring the answer back into the sheet.
    Adjust targets on Monthly Budget or cancel a subscription, then re-import next month to confirm the drift actually stopped.
You'll need

A free Claude or ChatGPT account, and your sheet open.

What you'll have
  • A plain-English read on your drift
  • An annualized cost of the leak
  • Three concrete actions
  • A way to verify next month
Watch out

The assistant only sees what you paste. Attach the current export and the _Schema tab — and leave out any account numbers you'd rather not share.

Build a Custom budget profile and save it

When none of the nine methodologies fits, build your own — and keep it.

  1. Run 💳 → Apply Budget Profile → Custom.
    The target cells turn yellow and become editable.
  2. Type your real numbers into the yellow cells.
    Total Budgeted and the free / over figures recalculate live as you type.
  3. Aim Unallocated toward zero.
    Watch the footer summary — if you're budgeting zero-based, get every dollar assigned a job.
  4. Save it: 💳 → Apply Budget Profile → Save current values as Custom…
    Your numbers now survive switching to another profile and back, and you can compare them against the presets anytime.
You'll need

A rough sense of your real monthly targets.

What you'll have
  • A budget that matches your life
  • Live totals as you edit
  • A saved Custom profile
  • Freedom to compare against presets
Watch out

Set targets to real, reachable numbers. A target that's unrealistically low makes every month read over and quietly drags down your Health Score's Budget Adherence.

Track a Goal and read the forecast

Set a target once; the sheet does the math and tells you what to change.

  1. Open Goals and start a new row.
    Type a name — Japan Trip Fund — and set Type to Savings Target.
  2. Set the linked account, Target, and Deadline.
    Enter a Target (say $6,000) and a date. Leave Current alone — it's pulled from the ledger, not typed.
  3. Let the progress fill itself in.
    The % complete bar and status chip update automatically as money lands in that account.
  4. Read the forecast strip and act — or don't.
    It reads like: "Hits target Oct 2026 — one month late. Bump $200 → $275 to hit September."
You'll need

A savings account linked on the Accounts tab.

What you'll have
  • A goal that tracks itself
  • No double-entry of balances
  • A live completion bar
  • A concrete fix to hit your date
Watch out

If a Goal's Current value looks wrong, it's a link problem, not a typo — confirm it points to the right account or category, and never edit the Current cell by hand.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

When it doesn't work — the fixes that cover ninety percent of support emails.

Setup & install
The 💳 Column & Co. menu didn't appear.
Reload the sheet (the menu builds on open). If it's still missing, open Extensions → Apps Script and confirm every .gs file pasted in and saved. The menu builds in onOpen().
Google is asking me to authorize. Is that safe?
Yes. The scope is spreadsheets.currentonly — the narrowest Google offers. The script can only touch this one workbook. It can't read your Drive or other sheets.
I opened the .xlsx and nothing works.
You're still in Excel-compatibility mode. File → Save as Google Sheets first, then install the script. The .gs layer only runs in Google Sheets.
Bank Import
My rows imported with a blank account.
The name in C10 didn't match an Accounts entry exactly. Fix the name, delete the blank rows, re-import.
Everything filed as Misc.
Your bank's header wasn't recognized, so categorization fell back to Misc. Confirm the CSV has a real header row, then add keyword rules on Categories and run Recategorize Ledger from Rules.
It imported the same month twice.
It shouldn't — the importer dedupes against the ledger. If you see dupes, you likely edited a Date or Amount, which changes the dedupe key. Delete by hand and re-import the original export.

Theme & Profile
Does Apply Theme change the header too?
Yes. Applying a theme repaints the entire sheet — the 3-row header chrome included — in the palette's colors. Only the wordmark and layout stay put. See Appendix A.
I switched profiles and lost my custom numbers.
You didn't — each profile keeps its own overrides. Switch back to that profile and they're there. They're only gone if you ran "Clear overrides."
A white-accent palette looks washed out.
Crimson & White and Forest & White use white accents on a near-white background — intentional, low-contrast by design. Pick a different palette if you want more punch.
Data & display
A grocery charge is showing up as income.
Amount sign. Expenses are negative, income positive. Flip the sign on that row in Transactions.
My Health Score Budget Adherence is low.
Usually your targets are off, not your spending. Revisit Monthly Budget — if a target is unrealistically low, every month reads "over."
A Goal's Current value looks wrong.
Current is pulled, not typed. Check that the goal is linked to the right account or category. Don't edit the Current cell directly.

Appendix D — Glossary

The brand and product terms used throughout this guide and inside the sheet.

AI Insights
The Dashboard panel that auto-computes a Leak, a Subs count, and a Win from your data — the LLM-ready wedge made visible.
Brand chrome
The 3-row letterhead atop every tab: header, sub-band, and 3px rule. Forest / Canopy / Gold by default — and retints with the active palette.
Canopy
The mid-green (#2D5C45). The second row of the chrome and the color of section labels.
Composite Score
The single 0–100 Health Score, a weighted blend of five indicators.
Custom profile
A budget profile you fill in by hand and save. Survives switching to other profiles and back.
Forest
The primary brand green (#1C3D2E). Headers, big numerals, footers, KPI cards.
Harvest Gold
The accent (#C5A95A). The logo spine, the chrome rule, key callouts — used sparingly.
_Engine
Hidden tab that pre-aggregates the ledger by month × category so dashboards stay fast.
_Schema
Hidden tab describing the workbook in plain English so an AI can read it.
Named range
A label pointing at a cell or block (e.g. budget_targets) so formulas survive layout changes.
Pacing bar
The progress bar on Top Spending and budget rows showing spent against target.
Profile
One of 10 budget methodologies that writes a full set of category targets.
Sparkline
A tiny in-cell line or bar chart — used on Trends, Net Worth, and budget shares.
Status chip
The On Track / Fair / Over pill, colored Canopy / Gold / Garnet.
Tile picker
The 24-tile palette grid on Start Here and the floating swatch button.
